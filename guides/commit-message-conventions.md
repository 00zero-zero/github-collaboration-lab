# Commit message conventions

A useful commit message explains the durable intent of a change.

Keep the subject concise and imperative, and use the body to explain why the change exists, important constraints, and consequences that are not obvious from the diff.

Reference issues or design decisions when they provide context, but do not make the reader depend on an unavailable conversation.

When a pull request is the primary review unit, commits should still remain understandable enough for blame, bisect, and revert workflows.
